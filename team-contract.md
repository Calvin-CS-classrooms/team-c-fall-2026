This contract defines the expectations and working practices our team agrees to follow throughout the CS 262 project. The contract may be revised later in the semester if the team agrees that changes are needed.

## Expectations

- Each member will contribute consistently throughout every 3-week sprint rather than leaving assigned work until the end.
- Each member should expect to spend approximately **3-5 per week outside of class** on the project when needed.
- Team members are expected to attend scheduled meetings and arrive on time.
- If a member expects to miss a meeting or deadline, they should notify the team as early as reasonably possible.
- Interviews, illness, competitions, work, family responsibilities, and other unexpected commitments will be accommodated when communicated early.
- If a member cannot complete an assigned task, they should communicate this before the deadline so the team can adjust responsibilities if necessary.
- Team members should keep assigned tasks and their status updated in the GitHub Project Board.
- We recognize that the project workload may increase near major deadlines and later phases of the semester.

## Communication

- Microsoft Teams will be our primary communication platform.
- Team members should respond to project-related messages within **24 hours** whenever reasonably possible.
- Messages involving blockers or questions that prevent another member from continuing work should be treated as higher priority.
- Important decisions, changes to assignments, blockers, and meeting information should be documented in Teams.
- If a discussion becomes too complicated for chat, the team should move it to a meeting or call.
- Communication should remain respectful and focused on solving the problem.

### Disagreement

- Disagreement about project ideas, designs, and implementation decisions is expected and can improve the final product.
- Members should critique ideas, code, designs, and proposals rather than criticizing people.
- Everyone should have an opportunity to explain their reasoning before major decisions are made.
- Personal attacks, insults, or dismissive behavior are not acceptable.
- If consensus cannot be reached, the team will compare options based on project requirements, technical feasibility, and team priorities.

## Meeting Schedule

- The team will hold one recurring meeting each week lasting approximately **30–60 minutes**.
- Recurring meeting time: **Thursdays at 6:30pm**
- Meeting format: **In person**
- Additional meetings may be scheduled when needed.
- The Project Manager will organize the meeting agenda and keep the meeting focused.
- Members who cannot attend should notify the team beforehand and review meeting decisions and assigned action items afterward.

## Responsibilities

Each 3-week sprint will include a Project Manager and a Lead Engineer.

### Project Manager
- Organizes and runs team meetings.
- Coordinates assignments and sprint priorities.
- Tracks progress and blockers.
- Makes sure responsibilities and deadlines are clearly communicated.
- Submits the required weekly team status report to Professor Norman.

### Lead Engineer
- Reviews Pull Requests.
- Provides constructive feedback on submitted code.
- Confirms that changes meet project requirements and testing expectations.
- Coordinates or performs merges into the appropriate branch.

### All Team Members
- Complete assigned tasks or communicate early when they cannot be completed.
- Use branches and Pull Requests appropriately when contributing code.
- Avoid making major changes directly to shared code without team coordination.
- Write and maintain appropriate tests for implemented functionality.
- Participate in collaborative development and code review when needed.
- Keep assigned GitHub Project Board tasks updated.
- Ask for help when blocked rather than remaining stuck without communicating.

Additional responsibilities may be assigned during each sprint depending on project needs.

## Equitable Contribution and Conflict Resolution

If a contribution or teamwork problem occurs, the team will follow these steps:

1. **Direct communication:** The affected member or members will first discuss the issue respectfully with the person involved.
2. **Clarify the cause:** The team will determine whether the issue involves workload, communication, technical difficulty, unclear expectations, missed deadlines, or another cause.
3. **Agree on a solution:** The team will establish a reasonable next step, such as clarifying the task, providing help, changing responsibilities, or establishing a new deadline.
4. **Team discussion:** If the problem continues, the Project Manager will bring the issue to the full team for discussion and documentation.
5. **Instructor involvement:** If repeated attempts to resolve the problem do not work, a member repeatedly fails to contribute or communicate, or the issue seriously affects the project, the team will contact Professor Norman.

The purpose of conflict resolution is to solve the problem, preserve respectful collaboration, and keep the project moving forward rather than assign blame.
